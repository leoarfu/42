MOBILE-09

In-Service Operations & Troubleshooting

Requires MOBILE-8 — you can't diagnose a system you can't yet describe end to end.

1. Handover failure modes specific to NTN

Ephemeris-prediction mismatch (satellite's actual position/timing drifts from broadcast ephemeris — rare but possible with stale data), handover initiated too late because the elevation-angle margin was cut too tight for the deployment's link budget, and beam-to-beam handover failures within a single satellite's coverage as spot beams narrow toward the coverage edge (MOBILE-3 §2's aperture account, at its geometric limit).

2. Link budget degradation — causes, symptoms

Rain fade and atmospheric attenuation (frequency-dependent — worse at higher bands, a reason lower-frequency transparent LTE D2D has some resilience advantage here over higher-frequency approaches), foliage/urban-canyon blockage given handset antennas have no directionality to work around obstruction, and satellite EIRP degradation from beam congestion when too many handsets compete for the same spot beam's power budget.

3. Timing/Doppler-related failure signatures

Residual frequency offset after pre-compensation (if satellite ephemeris or UE GNSS fix is degraded, the Doppler correction from MOBILE-4 §2.2 is based on stale or wrong data), and timing-advance errors compounding near the edge of a pass where geometry — and therefore required TA — is changing fastest per unit time.

4. Diagnostic approach — what to check first, in order

  1. Geometry first — is the UE actually within a satellite's coverage footprint and above minimum elevation, or is this simply a coverage gap, not a fault?
  2. Timing/Doppler second — check residual frequency/timing offset against expected values for the UE's position in the pass.
  3. Link budget third — rain fade, blockage, beam congestion — is the received signal below the threshold this deployment's link budget assumed?
  4. Core/signaling last — only once radio-layer causes are ruled out, check registration/handover signaling for core-side timer or configuration issues (MOBILE-6 §3).

This order matters: chasing a core-signaling fault before confirming basic geometry and link budget wastes time on the layer least likely to be the actual cause.

  • Ephemeris mismatch — stale/wrong satellite position data breaks predictive handover
  • Beam-edge degradation — link budget worst at coverage-radius edge, same geometry as MOBILE-3
  • Rain fade — frequency-dependent, worse at higher bands
  • Diagnostic order — geometry → timing/Doppler → link budget → core/signaling

  1. Given the diagnostic order above, argue for or against reordering "link budget" ahead of "timing/Doppler" as the second check instead of the third — under what operational conditions would that reordering catch problems faster?
