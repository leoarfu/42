MOBILE-02

The Direct-to-Device Landscape

Names and categories before physics. Every player below is solving the same problem — get a signal from orbit into an ordinary handset with no new hardware — and each has made a different, defensible tradeoff. This page is the map; MOBILE-8 revisits it once you can explain why each tradeoff was made.

1. What "direct-to-device" means, precisely

D2D/D2C means an unmodified consumer handset — no external antenna, no specialized terminal — connects straight to a satellite using (or adapting) standard cellular protocols. This is a different category from VSAT-class terminals (flat-panel phased arrays, dishes) covered in M6: those terminals have real antenna gain and real power budgets. A smartphone has neither. That single constraint — closing a link with a sub-1-dBi antenna and a battery-limited transmitter — is the organizing problem of this entire track.

2. The architecture taxonomy

Three buckets, and every player below sorts into one:

  • Bent-pipe / transparent, LTE-based. Satellite acts as a repeater — amplify and relay, no onboard demodulation. Standard LTE waveform, because it was designed for terrestrial towers and unmodified phones already speak it. Fastest to market; least standardized for the satellite hop itself.
  • Regenerative, 3GPP NR-NTN. Satellite carries onboard baseband processing (in the fullest case, an onboard gNB). Built to the standard's NTN extensions (MOBILE-4). Higher capability, higher cost, slower to deploy.
  • MSS narrowband. Legacy mobile-satellite-service spectrum (L-band, S-band), built for low-rate messaging and emergency use, not broadband. Bolted onto phones via OS-level integration rather than carrier roaming.

3. Player-by-player

3.1 Starlink Mobile (formerly Direct to Cell)

Bent-pipe / LTE bucket. ~650+ D2C-equipped satellites as of early-to-mid 2026, live in 22+ countries, ~10 million monthly active users, rebranded "Starlink Mobile" mid-2026. Partners with terrestrial MNOs (T-Mobile in the US, Veon/Kyivstar, OneNZ) rather than operating its own core — the satellite is a relay back to the partner's existing terrestrial 4G/5G core. Gen2 satellites are reported to target the actual 3GPP NR-NTN standard, aiming for 2027 VERIFY — a planned move out of the bent-pipe bucket.

3.2 IRIS²

Regenerative / NR-NTN bucket, per SpaceRISE program materials. Thales Alenia Space's program description explicitly names a 5G system, regenerative payload, active antennas, and an onboard processor as core scope items. Government-anchored, not consumer-D2D-first — the mobile/D2D capability sits inside a broader sovereign-communications mandate rather than being IRIS²'s primary commercial pitch the way it is for Starlink Mobile or AST SpaceMobile.

3.3 AST SpaceMobile

Broadband-class D2D bucket, standard cellular frequencies, large phased-array satellites (BlueBird series). FCC-authorized for 248 satellites; ~45 targeted in orbit by end of 2026. Carrier partnerships include AT&T, Verizon, and Vodafone. Has moved into defense-adjacent work via a Missile Defense Agency SHIELD program contract, alongside its commercial MNO roadmap — a dual-track positioning worth noting since it mirrors, at smaller scale, the government/commercial split you'll see again with IRIS².

3.4 Amazon Leo + Globalstar

Hybrid: broadband constellation (formerly Project Kuiper, renamed Amazon Leo) combined with an $11.6 billion acquisition of Globalstar's spectrum and infrastructure (L-band + S-band, ~25 MHz) to add D2D capability. ~390+ Amazon Leo satellites in orbit as of mid-2026; the Globalstar deal is expected to close in 2027, with D2D deployment beginning around 2028. This is the furthest from live of the major players — an announced capability layered onto an existing broadband constellation, not yet an operating D2D service.

3.5 Lynk Global + Omnispace + SES

Smallest of the broadband-class players — "cell-tower-in-space" positioning. SES became a strategic backer following a Lynk/Omnispace merger; targeting service start around mid-2027. Worth tracking mainly because of the SES connection — it links back to IRIS²'s operator consortium.

3.6 Skylo

Narrowband/IoT bucket, MSS-spectrum-based. Reports millions of active consumer devices already, mostly for messaging/IoT rather than broadband — a materially different market than Starlink Mobile or AST SpaceMobile.

3.7 Apple Emergency SOS (Globalstar-based, transitioning to Amazon Leo)

Narrowband bucket, deliberately limited by design — emergency-only, 911/SOS-class messaging, iPhone 14+, restricted to a defined set of countries. Not a broadband competitor and not trying to be; useful here mainly as the low end of the capability spectrum for comparison.

5. Why so many approaches — the underlying disagreement

The field hasn't converged because the players are answering different questions. Starlink Mobile optimized for speed to market: use the waveform phones already speak, partner with existing MNO cores, accept a smaller total-capability ceiling in exchange for being live in 2024 rather than 2029. IRIS² optimized for government-grade capability and sovereignty, accepting a much longer timeline and higher per-satellite cost. AST SpaceMobile bet on satellite size — bigger phased arrays, fewer satellites, better link margin per beam, at a harder manufacturing problem. Amazon bought its way past years of spectrum-licensing delay via Globalstar rather than developing D2D capability organically. None of these is obviously wrong; each is a coherent answer to "what do we optimize first," and that question is the one worth asking about any new entrant you read about later.

  • Bent-pipe (transparent) vs. regenerative — repeater vs. onboard processing
  • Starlink Mobile — bent-pipe LTE today, targeting NR-NTN via Gen2, ~2027
  • IRIS² — regenerative NR-NTN, government-anchored, SpaceRISE + Airbus DS/Thales Alenia Space
  • AST SpaceMobile — broadband D2D, large phased arrays, AT&T/Verizon/Vodafone + DoD SHIELD
  • Amazon Leo + Globalstar — broadband constellation + acquired MSS spectrum, D2D not yet live
  • Lynk + Omnispace — SES-backed, small scale, targeting 2027
  • Skylo — narrowband/IoT, MSS spectrum, live at scale
  • Apple SOS — narrowband emergency-only, Globalstar → Amazon Leo
